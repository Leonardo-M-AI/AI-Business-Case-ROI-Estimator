# AI Business Case: Customer Retention - Build vs Buy vs Status Quo

## Executive Summary

Based on a customer base of 7,043 users with a 26.5% churn rate, roughly
$121,290 in monthly revenue is at risk from churn. Comparing three approaches
to act on this risk - a manual retention team, a proprietary ML model, and an
external scoring API - the **Buy** approach delivers the highest ROI
(258,220% at 12 months) at the lowest monthly cost ($14), while **Build**
offers a strong middle ground with full control over the model (ROI 9,734%,
cost $370/month). Recommendation: start with **Buy** to validate the
retention strategy cheaply, and move to **Build** once data volume or
customization needs justify owning the infrastructure.

## 1. The Problem

- Churn rate: 26.5% across a base of 7,043 customers
- Customers at risk of churning per month: 1,866
- Monthly revenue at risk: $121,290
- This is a business problem, not just a technical one: every month of
  inaction costs six figures in recurring revenue, regardless of how accurate
  the underlying model is. The real question is which operating model turns
  that prediction into recovered revenue most efficiently.

## 2. The Three Options

| Scenario | Monthly cost | Net saving | ROI (12m) | Payback |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Status Quo (manual team) | $6,400 | $29,987 | 468.5% | 0.2 months |
| Build (own model) | $370 | $36,017 | 9,734.3% | 0.0 months |
| Buy (external API) | $14 | $36,373 | 258,220.3% | 0.0 months |

Numbers generated by `financial_model.py`, assuming a 30% retention
intervention success rate.

## 3. Trade-offs to Consider

- **Latency vs. cost**: the external API (Buy) is cheapest and fastest to
  deploy, but ties the business to a vendor's pricing and rate limits. Build
  offers predictable, fixed costs at higher volume and full control over
  latency.
- **Privacy / GDPR**: Buy sends customer data to a third party for scoring,
  which is acceptable at low volume with a compliant vendor but a compliance
  risk to revisit at scale. Build keeps all data in-house.
- **Scalability**: Buy's cost scales linearly with the customer base and
  could eventually exceed Build's fixed infrastructure cost. The break-even
  point should be recalculated as the customer base grows.

## 4. Recommendation

Start with the **Buy** approach: it has virtually no upfront cost and proves
whether the retention strategy actually works before committing engineering
time. Once monthly API costs approach or exceed the roughly $370/month cost
of running an in-house model, or if data privacy requirements tighten,
switch to **Build** for full control and long-term cost stability.

## 6. Underlying Technical Model

Based on [customer-churn-predictor](https://github.com/Leonardo-M-AI/customer-churn-predictor),
a Random Forest model with ROC-AUC 0.833 that correctly identifies 70.9% of
customers who actually churn.

## Limitations

The 30% retention success rate is an assumption, not a measured result. It
should be validated with an A/B test before being used for real budgeting.
Average revenue per customer ($65/month) is an estimate; actual ARPU should
replace it once available. The financial model does not account for seasonal
churn variation or the one-time cost of building the first version of the
retention model.
